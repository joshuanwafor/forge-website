Most people choose a workspace on vibes: the light was good, the coffee was nice, somebody friendly showed them around. Three weeks later they discover the call booths are always full.

Tours are sales pitches. Here is what to ask so yours is also an inspection.

## Before you go

**Work a full day there first.** Any space worth joining sells a day pass. Buy one, come on a Tuesday or Wednesday — never a quiet Friday — and do real work. Everything below is easier to judge from a chair than from a walkthrough.

## Infrastructure

1. **What backs up the power, and how fast?** Inverters that carry the floor instantly, or a generator with a gap? Ask what happens in the seconds between.
2. **Is the network on protected power?** Frequently it is not, which turns a brief outage into several minutes offline.
3. **Can I run a speed test right now?** Do it at 2pm on a full floor, not at 9am when you are the third person in.
4. **What are the actual upload speeds?** Downloads look fine everywhere. Uploads are what break your video call and your deploys.

## The room

5. **Where do people take calls?** Count the booths, then count the people. A ratio worse than one booth per fifteen desks means you will be taking calls in the stairwell.
6. **Is there a genuinely quiet area?** Not "quieter". Somewhere talking is actually not allowed.
7. **Sit in the chair for twenty minutes.** Adjustable height, lumbar support, no armrest jabbing your desk. You will spend more hours in it than in your bed.
8. **Where does the light come from?** Direct sun on your screen at 3pm is a permanent problem, and no amount of good design fixes it.

## The people and the terms

9. **What is the notice period, and what happens to my deposit?** Get it in writing. This is where flexible spaces are least flexible.
10. **Who else works here?** Not for networking — for noise. A floor of sales teams sounds nothing like a floor of engineers.
11. **How often are events, and are they in the main room?** Weekly events in the working area means weekly afternoons you cannot use.
12. **What happens when something breaks?** Ask for a recent example. The answer tells you more about the operator than any amenity list.

## The awkward ones

Two questions people avoid, both worth asking:

**"What is your occupancy right now?"** A space that is 30% full is pleasant and possibly precarious. A space that is 95% full is stable and possibly cramped. Either is fine if you know which you are buying.

**"Can I talk to a member you did not introduce me to?"** Then ask them what annoys them. Everybody has one thing. If they cannot think of it, they have not been there long.

## On price

Compare total monthly cost, not headline rate. Add up the plan, meeting-room hours you will realistically use, any printing or locker fees, and the transport difference between locations. The cheaper space that is 40 minutes further away is rarely cheaper.

And judge the rate against uninterrupted hours, not against desks. A ₦40,000 desk you can use for six good hours a day beats a ₦25,000 desk that gives you three.
